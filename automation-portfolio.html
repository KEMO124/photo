Sales order automation

The situation

Orders arrived by email — some as PDFs, some as spreadsheet attachments, some typed in the body of the message — each customer using their own layout and their own part codes. One person opened every message, re-typed each line into the ERP, checked stock, and typed a confirmation back. Peak days built a backlog, and typos surfaced later as short shipments and credit notes.

What we built

An intake pipeline that reads an incoming order in whatever format it arrives in, maps the customer's part codes to your SKUs, validates price, quantity breaks and stock, creates the order in the ERP, and sends the confirmation — routing only the lines it is not confident about to a human queue.

  • Per-customer code mapping that learns from each correction the team makes
  • Duplicate PO detection before an order is created twice
  • Price and minimum-order-quantity checks against the live price list
  • An exception queue, so nothing uncertain is ever posted silently

Database AI integration

The situation

Everything the business needed to know was already in the database — what a customer bought last year, which jobs were running late, which products carried the thinnest margin. Getting an answer meant asking the one person who could write the query, then waiting. Most questions simply went unasked, and decisions were made on memory.

What we built

A question-and-answer layer over the operational database. Staff ask in plain language; the system writes the query, returns the table or chart, and shows the query it ran so the answer can be checked. Access is read-only and scoped by role, so nobody sees data they should not, and nothing can be altered by asking.

  • Read-only connection — the layer can never write, update or delete
  • Row and column permissions inherited from existing roles
  • The generated query is shown with every answer, so results are auditable
  • Useful questions can be saved and scheduled as recurring reports
Before Ask the analyst→ Join the queue→ Query written→ Exported to spreadsheet [N days]
Now Ask in plain English→ Answer + query shown [N sec]

Quotation automation

The situation

Every quote started as last month's quote with the numbers changed. Pricing rules lived in one experienced head, so margins drifted depending on who was quoting; revisions overwrote each other, leaving nobody sure which version the customer was holding; and follow-ups happened when someone remembered.

What we built

A quoting engine that assembles the quote from live cost inputs and the company's own pricing and discount rules, produces the branded document, keeps every revision as a numbered version, sends it, and follows up on a schedule until the quote is won, lost, or expired.

  • Margin floors enforced at the point of quoting, with approval required to go below
  • Full version history — you can always see exactly what the customer received
  • Automatic follow-up sequence, stopping the moment the customer replies
  • Win/loss reasons captured and fed back into pricing decisions

Quality lab automation

The situation

Test results were written on a bench sheet, typed into a spreadsheet, and typed a third time into the certificate of analysis. Every transcription was a chance to introduce an error, specification breaches were often noticed only at certificate stage — sometimes after the batch had already moved — and reconstructing who tested what, when, and on which instrument meant going back through paper.

What we built

A bench-to-certificate system. Results are captured once, either straight from the instrument or on a tablet at the bench, and checked against the specification limits as they are entered. Anything out of spec is flagged immediately and the batch is held. The certificate of analysis is generated from the same record, so no number is ever re-typed.

  • Live specification checking at the point of entry, not at certificate stage
  • Automatic batch hold and notification on an out-of-spec result
  • Retest and deviation workflow recorded against the batch
  • Complete audit trail: analyst, timestamp, instrument, method revision

Now Capture at the bench→ Checked against spec instantly→ COA generated 1 entry, 0 re-typing

Result

  • [N hours/week of transcription removed]
  • [out-of-spec results caught at entry rather than at COA]

AI tax auditing engine

Why we built it

Reviewing a set of books or a return for errors is exactly the kind of work that eats expert hours: read every document, check every figure against the rules, and find what is missing, misclassified, or claimed twice. It is high-volume, high-stakes, and almost entirely mechanical — until it isn't. So we built the engine ourselves, as a product rather than a one-off, and it is where our own R&D on document understanding and rules engines lives.

How it works

Documents go in; findings come out. The AI does the reading — pulling figures out of receipts, statements, slips and ledgers, whatever shape they arrive in. A separate deterministic rules engine does every calculation and eligibility test. That split is the design decision the whole product rests on: a language model that does the arithmetic cannot be audited, and an audit tool that cannot be audited is worthless.

  • Extraction from mixed, messy source documents — scans, photos, exports, PDFs
  • Deterministic rules engine for all calculations and eligibility determinations
  • Discrepancy detection: figures that disagree, entries claimed twice, documents missing
  • Every finding cites the provision it rests on, in plain language
  • Every number traces back to the page and line it came from
Manual Read every document→ Key into working paper→ Check each rule by hand→ Write up findings [N hrs/file]
Engine Upload the file→ Extracted + tested against rules→ Cited findings, reviewer confirms [N min/file]

What it demonstrates

  • We build systems that show their working — every output is traceable to a source
  • We know where AI belongs in a workflow, and where deterministic code has to take over
  • We can carry a build from prototype through to a product with real compliance constraints
