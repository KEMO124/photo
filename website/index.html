The Shift

Why now — and what actually changed

The last decade put your business on software. This one puts software to work.

For twenty years, operational technology has been about the record. ERPs, CRMs, cloud spreadsheets, dashboards — they made information available. They did not make the work go away. Somebody still opens the email, downloads the export, reads the figure, retypes it into the next system, checks it against a rule they carry in their head, and passes it on. The gap between systems is where a company's hours quietly go.

What changed is that machines can now read unstructured documents, apply judgment inside defined rules, and explain what they did. That moves automation past rigid if-this-then-that scripting and into work that used to require a person's attention: reading an invoice, reconciling a figure, deciding whether an order should be held. The businesses installing this layer now aren't buying a tool — they're removing a permanent tax on their growth.

We build in that gap. Not demos, not pilots that die in a folder — systems that run every day, inside the operation, against real money and real deadlines.

Read

Emails, PDFs, exports, forms and legacy tables become structured, reliable data — without a person transcribing a single line.

Decide

Your rules, limits and tolerances applied identically every time — credit exposure, price integrity, spec conformance — with the reasoning recorded.

Act

Records written, documents generated, people notified — inside the systems your business already runs on, not beside them.

01Order to
confirmation

Sales Order Automation

An order arrives — a web form, an email, a marketplace notification, a PDF purchase order — and from that moment nothing needs to be retyped. The system reads it, validates it against your catalogue and available stock, applies the correct customer pricing and terms, and writes it into your ERP as a confirmed record.

Downstream, everyone sees the same thing at the same time: the warehouse gets a pick list, finance gets an invoice ready to issue, the customer gets a confirmation. Where an order fails a check — an unknown item, a price that doesn't match the quote, a customer past their credit limit, stock that isn't there — it is held and routed to a named person with the reason attached, instead of failing silently three steps later.

Receive→ Parse→ Validate→ Price→ Post to ERP→ Confirm

Order-to-confirmation falls from hours to minutes, and manual entry errors leave the chain entirely.

02Request to
quotation

Quotation Automation

A request for pricing shouldn't wait on whoever happened to build the last one. The system pulls live pricing, applies the customer's agreed discount structure, currency, lead time and validity terms, and produces a branded, ready-to-send quotation — complete, correct, and consistent with every quote before it.

Quotes are logged as they go out, so follow-ups fire on schedule and conversion becomes measurable rather than anecdotal. When the resulting order arrives, it is checked back against the rate that was actually quoted — closing the gap where margin quietly leaks between what was promised and what was invoiced.

Request→ Price lookup→ Terms & discounts→ Generate→ Send→ Track

Quote turnaround measured in minutes instead of days — priced the same way, every time, by everyone.

03Scattered to
single source

Database & AI Integration

Most businesses don't have a data problem, they have a location problem: the same fact lives in an ERP table, three spreadsheets, an inbox, and someone's memory. We consolidate scattered records into one clean, governed database — reconciled, de-duplicated, and continuously synchronised with the systems that feed it.

Then we put a language layer on top. Your staff ask a question in plain English — which customers are above eighty percent of their credit limit? what did we scrap on Line 2 last month? — and get an answer drawn from live data, with the underlying records attached so it can be checked. The same layer watches for what nobody thought to ask: anomalies, broken trends, thresholds crossed, raised on its own before they become a problem.

Connect→ Reconcile→ Govern→ Ask in plain language→ Monitor

One live source of truth that answers questions instantly and surfaces what deserves attention unprompted.

04Test to
certificate

Quality & Lab Automation

For businesses running lab testing, inspection or batch release, we take the paper out of the loop. Test results are captured at the point of measurement, logged against the correct batch, and evaluated automatically against your specification limits — pass, fail, or out of tolerance, applied identically every time regardless of who is on shift.

From there the system produces the documents the process actually requires: certificates of analysis tied to the right batch and shipment, non-conformance records, and compliance-ready reports. Quality managers get real-time visibility into what is in spec across the floor, instead of finding out at the end of the week.

Capture→ Log to batch→ Evaluate vs spec→ Certify→ Archive

Certificates in seconds rather than twenty minutes, with transcription error removed from the compliance record.

Process

Six stages. You see working software before you commit to a build.

How an engagement actually runs.

Automation projects fail in predictable ways: built against how a process is supposed to work rather than how it really works, or delivered as a demo that never survives contact with a live operation. Our sequence is designed to remove both risks early.

01

Discover

We walk the process as it actually runs — with the people who run it, at their desks. We watch the work, not the org chart. Most of what matters is in the exceptions nobody documented.

02

Map

Every step, decision, exception and system touchpoint written down, with the hours attached. You get a clear picture of where the time goes and what is worth automating first — before you spend anything on building.

03

Prototype

A working slice of the highest-value step, running on your real data, within days. You judge something real rather than a proposal, and you can stop here if it isn't right.

04

Build

Full engineering: system integration, validation rules, exception handling, access control, and the interface the team will genuinely use. Built to be maintained, documented as it's written.

05

Integrate & prove

Deployed against live operations and run in parallel with the manual process until the outputs match, every time, including the awkward cases. Only then does the manual process stop.

06

Operate & extend

Monitoring, support, and the next process in the queue. Systems earn their keep by staying correct as the business changes — so we stay accountable for that, or hand you everything needed to own it yourself.

Outlook

Where this is going — and how we build for it now

We build today's systems on the assumption that they will be asked to do far more.

AI capability is not arriving gradually. What required a specialist model and a research budget two years ago is now a component you can put into a production workflow. Any system built now that cannot absorb that curve will be replaced by one that can — so we build for what is coming, not only for what is currently possible.

NearAutomation → agency

From executing steps to holding a goal

Today's systems execute steps we define in advance. The next generation holds an objective — clear the order backlog, close the month, keep the line running — and chooses the steps itself, escalating only when it hits something outside its authority. That is powerful and it is dangerous in equal measure, which is why we build the boundaries first: what a system may decide alone, what it must ask about, what it can never do without a human. Get that structure right and increasing autonomy becomes a setting you turn up, not a rebuild.

CompoundingRecording → predicting

The automation pays for itself twice

Every automated process generates a clean record: run hours, downtime causes, scrap rates, payment behaviour, quote-to-close ratios, cycle times. A year of that is the raw material for forecasting — predicting machine failure before it stops production, a cash position before it tightens, demand before it arrives. Companies automating now aren't only buying back hours; they are accumulating the dataset that makes prediction possible later. Companies that wait will start that clock from zero.

StructuralJudgment as infrastructure

The rules in people's heads become an asset

Pricing logic, credit policy, tolerance limits, approval thresholds, the conditions under which an order gets held — in most businesses these live in the memory of a handful of experienced people, and walk out of the door when they do. Encoding them into systems does more than automate a task: it turns institutional judgment into something the company owns, inspects, improves deliberately, and applies consistently across every transaction and every new hire.

StrategicThe ownership question

Own the layer your operations run on

As more of a business runs on models it did not train and platforms it does not control, the question that matters is what you actually own. We build so that the data, the logic and the system stay yours: portable, inspectable, documented, and replaceable component by component as the technology moves. The model layer will keep changing — the operational logic you've encoded should outlive every model it runs on.
